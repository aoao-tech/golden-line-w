## Brand Identity

**Name:** Golden Line  
**Niche:** Restaurant delivery operations — customer churn & LTV loss  
**Tone:** Recognition + Curiosity (insightful, unexpected, not alarming)  
**Differentiator:** Focus on hidden LTV loss: customers who don't come back after 2 mistakes. Nobody else talks about this.  
**Mission:** Help restaurant owners recognize and understand customer churn patterns driven by repeat service failures.

---

## Primary Audience (ICP)

**Primary Segments:**
- Restaurant owners with own delivery operation (NOT Wolt/Uber-dependent)
- Suppliers/packers with own fulfillment (e.g., Vegetable Packaging House)
- Online food businesses with own CRM + fulfillment

**Core Pain Points:**
- Losing repeat customers after 2nd bad experience (silent LTV loss)
- No visibility of complaint history at preparation time
- Refund abuse by customers with patterns
- Information gap between CS team and fulfillment operations

**What They Want:**
- Reduce customer churn
- Prevent false/repeat refunds
- Systematic complaint handling at packing moment
- Lower delivery-related losses

## Proven Hook Patterns (from 6,037 avg views analysis)

### Pattern #1: Direct Lead with Data (TOP PERFORMER: 1.0 score)
**Example:** "86% of customers leave after 2 mistakes. But you only see the refund. You miss the customer who just stopped ordering."

**Why it works:**
- Specific, odd number (86% is more credible than "most")
- Data-first (no fluff)
- Reframes the problem (not refunds → churn)
- 6th-grade reading level (2x more views than complex language)

**When to use:** Lead hook, pattern recognition angles

---

### Pattern #2: POV Flip (Score: 0.95)
**Example:** "You refunded the order. But that customer never comes back."

**Why it works:**
- Takes what they think (refund = solved) and flips it
- Creates surprise moment
- Connects visible action (refund) to invisible consequence (churn)

**When to use:** Mid-content reframes, perspective shifts

---

### Pattern #3: Contrarian (Score: 0.85)
**Example:** "Most restaurants focus on refunds. The real loss is churn."

**Why it works:**
- Challenges common assumption
- Positions expertise (counter-intuitive insight)
- Not alarmist, just data-driven

**When to use:** Opening statements, content pillars

---

### Pattern #4: Problem-Solution (Score: 0.80)
**Example:** "Customers leave silently. Most restaurants never notice."

**Why it works:**
- Acknowledges gap (no visibility)
- Hints at solution (if restaurants could see...)
- Creates curiosity

**When to use:** Transition to benefits, setup for demo

## Copywriting Principles (from marketingskills)

### Clarity Over Cleverness
- If you have to choose, choose clear
- Golden Line is about insight, not wordplay

### Benefits Over Features
- Feature: "Shows complaint history at packing"
- Benefit: "Packer fixes it before shipping, customer doesn't churn"

### Specificity Over Vagueness
- ❌ "Save money on refunds"
- ✅ "86% of customers leave after 2 mistakes — recover them before they go silent"

### Customer Language Over Company Language
- Use: "packer," "bon," "complaint," "repeat customer"
- Avoid: "stakeholder engagement," "proactive remediation"

### Show Over Tell
- Don't say: "This is a problem"
- Show: "Customer #3 ordered 5 times. Two complaints. Never ordered again."

### Active Over Passive
- ❌ "The complaint history is shown to the kitchen"
- ✅ "The packer sees the complaint history"

### Confident Over Qualified
- ❌ "This might help reduce churn somewhat"
- ✅ "86% of customers leave after 2 mistakes. This fixes that."

## Visual Style Guidelines (for Higgsfield)

### Video Format
- **Visual Base:** Black background (simple, professional, minimal distraction)
- **Text Overlay:** White/light text, easy to read, animated
- **Duration:** 30-45 seconds (TikTok/Reels/Shorts standard)
- **Style:** Faceless, data-driven, no talking head needed

### What NOT to Show
- ❌ Generic restaurant kitchen footage (too common)
- ❌ Stock footage of sad people/unhappy customers (too cliché)
- ❌ Emotional/dramatic visuals (conflicts with "recognition, not alarm" tone)
- ❌ Cluttered dashboards or complex UIs without explanation

### What TO Emphasize
- ✅ Data visualization (numbers, charts, graphs)
- ✅ Comparative visuals (before/after, this vs that)
- ✅ Actual workflow moments (packing, bon, delivery)
- ✅ Timeline sequences (customer journey over time)
- ✅ Specific scenarios (real-world kitchen moments)

### Quality Standards
**Green Flags:**
- One sharp, unexpected insight per video
- Specific data or scenario (not generic advice)
- "Wait, I didn't know this" moment
- Directly connects to ICP pain (LTV loss, kitchen visibility)

**Red Flags (AVOID):**
- Generic restaurant advice (10 ways to improve customer service)
- Lists (5 ways, 7 tips — lazy format)
- No specific problem or data
- Obvious/already-known information

## Writing Formula for Scripts

### Opening (Hook) — 0-3 seconds
Use ONE of the proven patterns:
- Direct data lead: "86% of customers leave after 2 mistakes."
- POV flip: "You refunded it. But they never came back."
- Contrarian: "Most restaurants focus on refunds. Wrong."
- Problem-solution: "Your customers leave silently."

### Setup — 3-15 seconds
Provide context or scenario that makes the hook real:
- Specific number (86%, 3 months, $XXX)
- Real scenario (packing moment, repeat customer, CS team)
- Data point (your competitor is doing this, most restaurants miss this)

### Payoff — 15-30 seconds
Flip the perspective or reveal the insight:
- Reframe the cost (not refund, but churn)
- Highlight the opportunity (if you could see it, you could fix it)
- Validate the pain (you're not alone in this)

### CTA — 30-45 seconds
Comment if true, DM if interested, or signal next action

## Tone Checklist for Every Script

Before approving any Higgsfield generation, check:

- [ ] **Specific, not generic** — Does it reference 86%? A scenario? Not "customer service tips"?
- [ ] **Data-first** — Does it lead with a number or insight, not a question?
- [ ] **Unexpected** — Would a restaurant owner think "wait, I didn't know this"?
- [ ] **Kitchen-centered** — Does it reference the packing moment, the packer, or the operational gap?
- [ ] **No alarm** — Is it recognition + curiosity, not fear-mongering?
- [ ] **Action-oriented CTA** — Does it ask for comment or DM, not just "learn more"?
- [ ] **Customer language** — Are we using "packer," "complaint," "order," not corporate jargon?
- [ ] **6th-grade reading level** — Can a busy restaurant owner understand in 30 seconds?
